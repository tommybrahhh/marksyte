Thesis

Distribution compounds relevance. It does not create it from zero.

More doors increase availability, but also increase slotting exposure, trade spend, deductions, working capital, service pressure and the cost of a weak proposition.

Distribution economics

Distribution is expensive because it buys uncertainty.

Retailers and distributors are not only charging for shelf space. They are pricing the labor, system changes, risk, returns and opportunity cost of replacing one SKU with another.

Cost stack

Every new door adds claims on the same retail price.

Distribution only compounds when velocity and margin absorb the cost of access.

Market examples

Successful brands did not treat distribution as the first proof.

The strongest cases built a consumer reason, distinctive memory and early velocity before bigger retail systems were asked to carry the brand.

Brand Signal Lesson

04

Timing model

Scale only when pull and economics are both ready.

A brand can be exciting and still unready for mass distribution. It can also be operationally efficient but too invisible to earn shelf turns. The timing call sits in the intersection.

Consumer pull
Retail economics
High pull / low economics

Fix before mass retail

Negotiate pack size, COGS, freight, promo rules and price ladder.

High pull / high economics

Scale deliberately

Add doors by cluster, protect service levels and track velocity weekly.

Low pull / low economics

Stay in discovery

Do not buy distribution to answer a proposition question.

Low pull / high economics

Create demand first

Use sampling, content, communities and narrow doors to create proof.

06

Execution

A 90-day low-investment plan.

The goal is not to make the brand look scaled. The goal is to collect the proof that makes scale cheaper.

Weeks 1-2

Define the wedge

Pick one consumer, one occasion, one hero SKU and one reason to choose.

Weeks 3-6

Generate live demand

Run sampling and content together. Every sample should capture a response path.

Weeks 7-10

Test a small retail loop

Place the product in 5-20 matched doors and measure weekly velocity and feedback.

Weeks 11-12

Decide the next bet

Scale, pause, fix margin, refine proposition or change channel based on evidence.

Go when...

The product repeats, margin stays positive after real trade costs, the use case is easy to explain, retail velocity beats the agreed benchmark and the brand can keep shelves filled without starving cash.

Do not go when...

Growth depends on promotion, the consumer cannot explain the difference, the only KPI is door count, or the distributor math works only if every store performs like the best one.
