Sydney Airport uses local brands to renew duty free

Sydney Airport and Heinemann are beginning an 18-month transformation. MECCA shows how a trusted local retailer can bring curation, service and globally relevant brands.

Heinemann Oceania and Sydney Airport have announced an 18-month transformation of the main duty-free categories in Terminal 1. MECCA will be the most visible addition, but the programme covers beauty, liquor, tobacco, confectionery, arrivals, gate shops and an expanded Hermes boutique.

In brief
  • MECCA will enter international duty free for the first time through a standalone store in the new beauty zone.
  • The project adds Diptyque, Byredo, Emma Lewisham, Westman Atelier and Glossier, alongside MECCA labels and services.
  • The strategy aims to convert more passengers into shoppers through an offer closer to Australian consumers and Gen Z.

The key pointLocal identity does not require an exclusively local assortment. A trusted curator can select global brands, services and trends that are more relevant to passengers.

What is happening

The plan will completely redevelop liquor, tobacco and confectionery, overhaul beauty and refurbish arrivals and gate stores. It is due to finish in mid-2027 and has an explicit objective: grow sales for both airport and operator.

MECCA will open a standalone shop in the new beauty zone. The company serves more than four million customers a year, operates over 110 stores in Australia and New Zealand and works with more than 200 brands.

Its arrival adds Diptyque, Byredo, Emma Lewisham, Westman Atelier and Glossier, alongside MECCA COSMETICA, MECCA MAX and kit. A curated selection of beauty services will be offered. MECCA already operates in Sydney's domestic Terminal 3, but this will be its first international duty-free presence.

The wider transformation includes a 73 square metre Aesop boutique opened in May and a 120 square metre Victoria's Secret store, marking the brand's first entry into Australian duty free.

Why Sydney wants a stronger local identity

International traffic is growing and changing

Sydney Airport handled a record 17.17 million international passengers in 2025. Another 4.57 million travelled in the first quarter of 2026, up 5.8%. China grew 14%, Hong Kong 21.4%, Kuala Lumpur 32.3% and Guangzhou 38.5%.

This mix requires an international offer that is also relevant to Australians travelling abroad. Local identity can act as a trust signal for residents and a discovery experience for visitors.

Duty free needs to compete with familiar retailers

Consumers compare the airport shop with the experiences they know in cities and online. MECCA brings reputation, customer knowledge, exclusive brands, services and loyalty. The opportunity is not to copy a city store, but to adapt its proposition to time, baggage and travel.

Beauty is shifting towards service and curation

Too much choice can create friction. A curator reduces complexity, presents trends and helps customers decide. Makeup, skin and fragrance services can increase interaction, provided they fit different dwell times.

What it means for tourism and travel retail

The case challenges the idea that duty free should look the same everywhere. Standardisation supports buying and operations, but can remove reasons to explore. Sydney is combining Heinemann's scale with a recognisable local voice.

For global brands, entering through MECCA provides access to a curator with credibility in Australia. For Australian brands, the airport can act as an international showcase. The selection needs to balance familiarity, novelty and exclusivity.

A beauty store with services needs appointments, trial zones, expert staff, coordinated inventory and rapid collection. Technology can allow travellers to book before departure or recommend a service based on time to boarding.

For the destination, retail forms part of Australia's first and last impression. Aesop, local products and Australian design can strengthen sense of place without turning the terminal into a souvenir shop.

Design a relevant offer

Risks and practical barriers

  • Confusing a local brand with local product. MECCA's value lies in trust and curation, not in selling only Australian labels.
  • Copying the city store. Travellers have less time, limited baggage and different needs.
  • Adding services without capacity. Consultations can create queues when they ignore boarding time.
  • Failing to prove incrementality. Some sales may shift from the previous offer.
  • Losing alignment between partners. Airport, Heinemann, MECCA and brands need common objectives.

The project is due to finish in mid-2027. Conversion, incremental sales, basket and margin results have not yet been published.

Operational implications

Passenger analysis

Connect nationality, residence, route, frequency and shopping mission.

Assortment curation

Prioritise brands and products by affinity, novelty, margin and availability.

Service measurement

Connect appointments, interaction, conversion, basket and satisfaction.

Demand forecasting

Adjust inventory and staffing to flights, seasons and launches.

Retail media

Measure campaigns through incremental sales rather than exposure alone.

AI assistants

Recommend products and services according to time, destination and need.

AI can personalise choice, but the main advantage remains human: credible selection, expert service and a recognisable proposition.

A practical 90-day agenda

  1. Define the baseline. Measure traffic, entry, conversion, basket, margin and brands before the change.
  2. Segment the traveller. Separate Australians, visitors, Gen Z, routes and available time.
  3. Test short services. Compare five, ten and twenty-minute formats.
  4. Measure incrementality. Distinguish new sales, substitution between brands and traffic effects.

Sydney Airport is using a local brand to renew duty free, but the move goes beyond where brands come from. It uses local knowledge, trust and curation to make a global offer more relevant.

Frequently asked questions

Will MECCA replace the whole duty-free beauty offer?

No. MECCA will operate a standalone store in the new beauty zone. The wider project includes other brands, services and a full category redevelopment.

What does using a local brand mean in duty free?

It does not mean selling only domestic products. MECCA acts as an Australian retailer and curator of global brands, trends, services and its own labels.

Is the commercial impact already known?

No. The project finishes in mid-2027 and conversion, incremental sales, basket and margin results have not been published.
