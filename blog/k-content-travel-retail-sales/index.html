From K-drama to cart: how K-content creates travel-retail demand

A scene in the Goblin tenth-anniversary special turned a sunscreen with no boutique or physical display into a Shinsegae Duty Free digital bestseller. The case shows how cultural demand can arrive before the commercial forecast.

Between 1 and 19 July, sales of Da Effect Hydro Sunscreen at Shinsegae Duty Free rose by about 840% against the same period in June. Total brand sales increased by around 560%. The trigger was not a boutique opening, a major campaign or a price cut. It was a natural television appearance.

In brief
  • Actor Kim Go-eun used the sunscreen during the Goblin anniversary special.
  • Viewers searched communities and social platforms, taking formed demand directly to duty-free ecommerce.
  • The brand had no physical footprint at Shinsegae, yet recorded stock shortages and a growing share of Chinese and Southeast Asian buyers.

The key point K-content can create purchase intent outside the commercial calendar. Advantage does not necessarily belong to the largest brand. It belongs to the operator that identifies the signal, has the product available and makes immediate purchase possible.

What is happening

During the Goblin 10th Anniversary Trip programme, Kim Go-eun appeared applying Hydro Sunscreen. The moment prompted questions about the product’s identity, online searches and social discussion. Shinsegae Duty Free subsequently reported the sales jump and shortages in its digital store.

The channel is the most important detail. Da Effect did not operate a standalone boutique or receive physical display at Shinsegae. It was available exclusively online. Travellers identified the product through content, searched for it and reached the retailer with intention already formed.

Seasonality also helped. Sunscreen appeared as travellers were buying summer-holiday essentials. The result should not be attributed to the screen alone. The cultural moment activated a need with immediate travel relevance and a product format suited to duty-free purchase.

Why K-content can convert so quickly

The appearance looks like use rather than advertising

A demonstration embedded in a story narrows the gap between product and everyday life. The viewer sees who uses it, when and how naturally. This can provoke greater curiosity than advertising clearly separated from the content.

The community identifies products in real time

Forums, social platforms, visual search and creators turn a brief scene into a collective product page. Name, brand, price and seller can circulate before the company launches official communication. Demand begins to organise outside the brand.

K-beauty already has global infrastructure

Korean cosmetics exports reached US$10.2 billion in 2024. Ecommerce, distributors, marketplaces and international retailers allow cultural attention to become an order. Without existing distribution, interest would remain interest.

Travel creates urgency and context

Duty-free retail combines digital pre-order, rapid collection, price incentives and a confirmed trip. When a product becomes desirable shortly before departure, the channel can offer a faster route than waiting for international physical distribution.

What it means for tourism and travel retail

The case challenges forecasting based only on history, flights and promotion calendars. Those inputs remain essential, but they cannot see a scene that changes SKU demand within days. Forecasting needs an external-signal layer.

For retailers, ecommerce is not a secondary channel. It is infrastructure able to capture a trend without waiting for a boutique negotiation, staff training or a store refit. Product pages, internal search, availability and collection become rapid-response assets.

For brands, K-content can lower the cost of discovery while reducing control. Consumers may identify a product before translated materials, international stock or customer support exist. A reputation opportunity can become frustration when searches lead to counterfeits or unauthorised sellers.

For airports and destinations, culture and commerce move closer together. Filming locations, concerts, food and celebrities can influence the products visitors seek and the districts or stores added to an itinerary. Retail and tourism promotion can share signals without presenting content as advertising.

Risks and practical barriers

  • Confusing virality with durable demand. The peak may fall before replenishment arrives.
  • Buying too much too late. A delayed response can leave excess inventory.
  • False attribution. Season, promotion and content may act at the same time.
  • Uneven availability. Global interest does not guarantee registration or distribution in each market.
  • Counterfeits. Intense search can direct consumers to unauthorised sellers.
  • Celebrity dependence. The product needs a proposition that outlasts the cultural moment.
  • Content rights. Retailers and brands cannot reuse scenes or images without permission.
  • Fragmented data. Producer, platform, network, retailer and brand see different parts of the journey.

Shinsegae published sales growth and stock shortages but not units, absolute revenue, margin, programme audience or the expected duration of the effect. This article does not estimate those variables.

A practical 90-day agenda

  1. Define sensitive products. Prioritise visual, seasonal and K-content-linked SKUs.
  2. Create thresholds. Set alerts for search, mentions, visits and sales velocity.
  3. Prepare the response. Agree stock, content, languages, media and substitutes.
  4. Connect systems. Integrate external signals, ecommerce, flights, orders and availability.
  5. Measure the tail. Analyse how long the peak lasts and what value remains.

K-content does not replace commercial planning. It introduces a new source of volatility and opportunity. The winner will not be the company that tries to manufacture every cultural phenomenon, but the one able to recognise, supply and convert it with discipline.

Frequently asked questions

Was the Da Effect appearance a paid campaign?

The cited reports describe a natural product appearance during the Goblin anniversary special and position its effect beyond traditional endorsement. They do not publish an advertising agreement, campaign investment or independently measured exposure.

Can a small brand reproduce this growth?

There is no reliable formula for creating virality. A brand can prepare for it through searchable product data, digital distribution, safety stock, search alerts, multilingual content and rapid processes for media and merchandising activation.

What should a retailer measure beyond sales?

Searches, mentions, product-page visits, conversion, time to purchase, nationality, stock-outs, substitution, margin, repeat purchase and later brand sales. Organic content, paid activation and seasonality should be separated where possible.
