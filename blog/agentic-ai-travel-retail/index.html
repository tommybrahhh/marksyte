Agentic AI wants to book the trip: what changes for travel retail

AI assistants are moving from answering questions to comparing options, making decisions and taking action. For tourism and airport retail, competition may begin long before the passenger reaches the terminal.

Artificial intelligence is moving beyond travel inspiration. Agentic systems can interpret a goal, consult several sources, compare options and use tools to complete a task. In travel, that task may involve building an itinerary, tracking a fare, booking a hotel, changing a flight or arranging an in-trip service.

In brief
  • AI could become the interface that organises the whole journey, rather than a better search box
  • Trust, payments, live availability and support when something fails still constrain autonomous booking
  • Travel retail needs to compete for visibility before the passenger reaches the airport

The key point

Once an agent can understand the traveller's mission and execute the next step, commercial power shifts towards the system controlling data, recommendation logic and access to the transaction.

What is happening

The threat is now part of the strategic discussion at the largest travel intermediaries. On 20 July 2026, the Financial Times examined Expedia's response to autonomous agents that can plan and book trips. Four days later, Reuters reported the concern in direct commercial terms. Search, comparison and aggregation, which sit at the centre of the online travel agency model, are tasks an AI agent is designed to perform.

The shift is visible in operating products. Booking.com has linked language models to proprietary information on pricing, availability, policies and reviews. Its AI Trip Planner grew from a prototype launched in ten weeks. Omio connects conversational interfaces to live inventory covering rail, coach, ferry and air travel. Expedia Group B2B is developing components that allow other brands to embed travel supply and services inside their own AI workflows.

This does not mean autonomous agents already complete most bookings. Expedia's research with YouGov shows a clear gap between planning and purchase. Travellers are willing to use AI for suggestions, itineraries and price monitoring, yet many still prefer to transact with a familiar brand. The transition is likely to be gradual and hybrid. An agent may research and recommend while an airline, hotel, OTA or payment platform owns checkout, servicing and accountability.

Why it is happening now

Conversation handles complex intent

Traditional search works when the customer knows the destination, dates and product. Many journeys begin with a less precise request, such as a warm short break without crowds, a quiet hotel near public transport or a practical gift that can be collected before departure. Conversation can combine budget, timing, preferences, restrictions and context in one request.

Transaction infrastructure is already available

The language model is only the visible layer. A usable recommendation needs inventory, fares, schedules, policies, identity, payments and service. Expedia Group B2B says its platform handles 21 billion API calls per day and supports 75,000 partners. Infrastructure of this scale allows an agent to move from generic advice to an option that is live, eligible and bookable.

Distribution is spreading beyond travel websites

A booking opportunity can appear inside a general assistant, mobility app, card programme, loyalty environment or airport platform. Each new interface competes to capture intent before the traveller opens an OTA or visits an airline website.

What it means for tourism and travel retail

The first change is about timing. Commercial decisions move earlier. An agent can recommend insurance, transfers, an eSIM, lounge access, baggage, food or travel products while assembling the itinerary. By the time the passenger reaches the airport, some choices may already have been made.

The second change concerns visibility. A physical store gives shelf space to a range of products. A conversational answer may present three. Product attributes, availability and ranking rules become a new form of merchandising. Retail media changes too. A brand may pay for visibility, but the recommendation must remain relevant, disclosed and capable of producing incremental sales rather than paid exposure alone.

The third change is analytical. Conversations reveal intent before the transaction. Questions about weather, baggage, children, connections, health or gifting can provide early demand signals by route and occasion. These signals do not replace bookings, flight schedules or sales. They enrich them and can shorten the time between a market change and a commercial response.

Opportunities by company type

FMCG and brands

Make products readable by AI

Structure attributes, pack roles, occasions, restrictions, claims, price and availability. Build bundles around destination, trip length, climate and mission.

Retailers and duty free

Compete before the terminal

Connect catalogue, stock, eligibility, collection and promotions with assistants that only recommend fulfilable options.

Airports

Become the context layer

Provide terminal location, security time, distance, opening hours, disruption and commercial capacity.

Airlines

Join travel and commerce

Use booking, check-in, connection and disruption context to present relevant services with consent and clear rules.

Hotels

Sell beyond the room

Expose policies, amenities, food, wellness, mobility and experiences as structured, combinable services.

Destinations

Influence discovery

Improve data on seasonality, access, events, neighbourhoods and alternatives so recommendations fit the traveller's intent.

Services and payments

Enter the itinerary

Integrate insurance, eSIM, mobility, finance, tax refund and assistance into a coherent travel proposition.

Technology and retail media

Build the decision layer

Connect identity, feeds, inventory, rules, attribution, sponsored content and handover to human support.

Risks and barriers

  • Incorrect recommendations. An outdated price, stock position, policy or restriction can invalidate the whole experience.
  • Opaque influence. Travellers need to distinguish organic relevance from paid promotion and commercial relationships.
  • Fragmented accountability. When the agent, platform, supplier and payment provider are different organisations, problem resolution can become unclear.
  • Privacy and control. Personalisation improves with trip, loyalty and purchase data, but requires clear purpose, minimisation and consent.
  • Platform dependency. Companies may lose direct traffic, customer data and margin if they surrender the interface without a distribution strategy.
  • Availability bias. An agent may favour the easiest inventory to integrate rather than the best option for the traveller or destination.
Adoption should not be judged by conversations or clicks alone. The scorecard needs accurate availability, completed booking, margin, cancellations, support demand, satisfaction and incrementality.

Operational implications

Demand forecasting

Combine flights, bookings, sales, searches and conversational signals to forecast demand by route, category and time.

Route and destination analysis

Estimate how new services, connections and AI recommendations change the value of each origin and market.

Traveller segmentation

Translate intent, mission, time and context into actionable segments that go beyond demographics.

Assortment, inventory and pricing

Prioritise products, packs, stock and promotions according to expected demand, margin and fulfilment confidence.

Retail media and personalisation

Design visibility rules, separate organic and sponsored recommendations and measure incremental value.

Automation and AI assistants

Build controlled prototypes for planning, recommendation, support and internal analysis with human escalation.

Operational planning

Connect the forecast with staffing, opening hours, replenishment, collection points and disruption response.

Commercial impact measurement

Use experiments, control groups and attribution models to separate convenience, cannibalisation and true growth.

The aim is not to automate every decision. It is to identify where AI removes friction, where it improves precision and where a person or trusted brand should remain in control.

Those that do not may remain present in the market while disappearing from the decision.

Frequently asked questions

How is agentic AI different from a travel chatbot?

A chatbot answers or recommends. An agentic system can break a goal into steps, consult tools, compare alternatives and take actions such as monitoring prices, booking or changing a service within defined controls.

Will agentic AI replace online travel agencies and airport retail?

There is no evidence for complete replacement. Search and comparison face greater pressure. Businesses that own trusted inventory, service, fulfilment and transaction capability can still capture value even when the interface changes.

What should a travel retail business do first?

Audit product, price, stock, eligibility, location and collection data. Then test one specific traveller mission and connect recommendation quality with conversion, margin and fulfilment.
